I'm a professor at York University, where I teach astronomy and
space-based science to students from across the university, many of
whom are not science majors. My courses include introductory
astronomy, the history of astronomy, and exploring the solar
system.

My research focuses on quasars: supermassive black holes, billions
of light-years away, that are actively feeding on the gas around
them. As they feed, they become some of the brightest objects in
the universe, which lets us study them across enormous distances
and learn how black holes and galaxies grow together.

Outside the classroom, I regularly give public talks, take part in
community events, and comment on astronomy and space science
stories in the media. Journalists and event organizers are welcome
to get in touch by email.
